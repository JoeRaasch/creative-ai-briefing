01 · Entertainment business

Paramount–WBD is still waiting on the judge after the settlement gets fresh scrutiny.

U.S. District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín did not approve the settlement that would clear Paramount Skydance’s Warner Bros. Discovery takeover at Thursday’s hearing. She asked the parties to respond by Monday to Sen. Cory Booker’s request for an independent public-interest review and allowed outside groups to file briefs, saying a ruling would come in “due course.”

The proposed settlement still contains meaningful production floors: at least 30 theatrical releases a year for the first two years, 32 in years three through five, and at least $300 million in additional U.S. film production spending each year. Paramount separately said it now plans to raise about $44.4 billion of additional secured debt for the takeover and related refinancing.

Why it mattersThe merger is not closed yet. If it does close, two major buyers of film, television, VFX and animation work become one; the five-year production commitments put a floor under part of that activity, but they do not remove the longer-term consolidation risk or the pressure to find savings across overlapping businesses.

02 · Games / generative AI

Meta’s new Horizon tools turn prompts into publishable mobile games.

Meta introduced Horizon Create, a phone app, and Horizon Studio, a browser editor that use the Horizon Engine’s agentic tools to build 2D or 3D games from natural-language descriptions. Meta says the system can generate environments, characters, mechanics, progression, difficulty and multiplayer, while Studio adds direct scene editing, asset replacement and script-parameter controls.

Projects can move between the two tools, and published games are eligible for native distribution across Facebook, Instagram and Horizon. Access is still gradual and waitlist-based; Meta has not yet published full monetization details.

Why it mattersThis is less interesting as an Unreal or Unity replacement than as a new low-friction prototyping and distribution layer. For game students and small teams, the useful test will be how much control survives after the first prompt and whether generated projects remain understandable enough to revise rather than merely regenerate.

03 · ComfyUI / production

Comfy Router puts major media models behind one API without hiding the provider.

Comfy Router is now live on the Comfy Developer Platform, providing one API for hosted image, video, 3D and audio models including Seedance 2.5, MiniMax H3, Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 2, Kling and Black Forest Labs models. Where a model is available from more than one backend, users can explicitly choose among providers such as fal, Runware, WaveSpeed and Higgsfield; Comfy says it does not silently switch a named provider.

The same API also provides queued jobs and shared run history, with inputs and outputs deleted after 24 hours. Comfy explicitly notes that direct integration is still the simpler option when a pipeline depends on only one provider and one model.

Why it mattersFor a studio or lab using several hosted generative models, provider switching and rate-limit handling become pipeline problems quickly. Router reduces that integration work while preserving a useful bit of control: you can still know which provider ran the job instead of treating routing as a black box.

04 · Local / open AI

Liquid’s small draft model speeds up vision-language inference on Apple silicon and GPUs.

Liquid AI released LFM2.5-VL-DSpark, an experimental 279.5M-parameter draft model that accelerates its 3B vision-language model through speculative decoding. Liquid reports 1.56–2.62× end-to-end speedups on an M5 Max, 1.30–1.77× on an M3 Ultra, and 1.64–2.27× on an H100 across its measured vision tasks; those are vendor benchmarks using 16-bit weights.

The drafter is available with support in MLX-VLM, llama.cpp and SGLang. It does not speed up the vision encoder or prompt prefill, so the benefit shrinks when those stages dominate the workload.

Why it mattersLocal VLMs are useful for image inspection, tagging, UI understanding and asset triage, but interactive latency can make them unpleasant to use. This is a practical optimization rather than a new model capability, and it is relevant to workstation-class Apple hardware as well as datacenter GPUs.

05 · Blender / Gaussian splats

Blender 5.3 makes Gaussian splats a native scene type.

Blender 5.3 now has native import and rendering support for 3D Gaussian Splats in its current alpha builds. PLY and SPZ captures come in as a dedicated point-cloud type, Geometry Nodes can load and modify them, and Workbench, EEVEE and Cycles can render them without a third-party splat add-on.

The implementation is not finished: there is no splat export, performance is still flagged as suboptimal, Cycles and EEVEE have color limitations around low-opacity/high-radiance data, and Apply Transform does not yet handle all splat attributes correctly.

Why it mattersThis gives captured environments a direct route into the same scene as Blender cameras, lights, geometry and animation. For now it looks best suited to look development, previs and experimentation rather than a complete interchange pipeline, but native Geometry Nodes access makes the direction much more useful than a viewer-only implementation.
