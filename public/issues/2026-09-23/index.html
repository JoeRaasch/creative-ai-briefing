01 · Entertainment business

Paramount–WBD has one more court check before the merger can close.

Paramount Skydance and 12 state attorneys general have agreed on terms to settle the antitrust case blocking its Warner Bros. Discovery takeover, but the settlement is not final yet. U.S. District Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín says she still has questions about the proposed consent decree and has scheduled a September 24 hearing before deciding whether to dissolve the no-close order; Paramount CEO David Ellison has told staff he expects the transaction to close in roughly two weeks.

The proposed settlement would require the combined company to release at least 30 theatrical films a year for its first two years and 32 in years three through five, while spending at least $300 million more per year on U.S. film production than Paramount and WBD spent in 2025.

Why it mattersThe production floors are meaningful because they push against the simplest version of the consolidation fear: that the combined studio will immediately make fewer films. The longer-term risk remains that two major buyers become one, with integration decisions affecting overlapping jobs, vendor leverage and how many separately financed slates compete for VFX, animation and production crews.

02 · Frontier models / agents

OpenAI and Anthropic cut the cost of capable agent models on the same day.

OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and Luna at $2/$10 and $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens respectively, half the promotional API pricing of their GPT-5.6 counterparts, while cached GPT-6 input receives a 90% discount. Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens with $0.20 cache reads; Anthropic says typical workloads cost about 40% less than Opus 5 because the new model also uses fewer tokens per task.

Both companies published strong benchmark claims, but the cleaner comparison today is price and availability rather than small score differences measured under different harnesses.

Why it mattersFor persistent creative agents—batch metadata, asset triage, DCC automation, script-to-shot tools or coding—the bill for repeated calls matters as much as peak benchmark rank. More capable workhorse models at lower per-task cost make sustained automation easier to justify than another expensive ceiling model.

03 · Creative software

Premiere arrives on Android as a free 4K editor.

Adobe has released Premiere mobile for Android with a multitrack timeline, frame-accurate editing, Enhance Audio, 4K editing and export, no watermark and no required Adobe login for basic editing. The launch requires Android 13 or later and at least 5GB of RAM; keyframes, beat detection, cropping, improved captions and desktop project handoff are listed as coming later.

Adobe’s launch post also advertises Firefly-powered Generative Fill, Image-to-Video and generated sound effects. Its product and support pages are still inconsistent about Android generative-AI availability, so those features are worth checking on the actual device before treating them as part of the Android launch.

Why it mattersFor field work and classroom use, free 4K editing without a watermark or mandatory account is the useful part. The AI pitch can wait until Adobe’s documentation and the shipping Android feature set clearly agree.

04 · Local / open agents

Laya turns agent routing into a 322–421M parameter local model.

Laya 0.3.6 landed on PyPI on September 22 with Apache-2.0 checkpoints for typed decisions such as label choice, ordinal scoring and yes/no probability. The project reports roughly 33ms single-question latency on a T4 for its multilingual checkpoint and avoids text generation entirely, returning structured decisions in one forward pass.

The repository is unusually clear about its limits: the base checkpoints sit near chance on its typed-decisions benchmark, the headline 0.766 result comes from a checkpoint fine-tuned on that benchmark’s training split, and high-cardinality label sets still degrade sharply.

Why it mattersA small router could decide which model, tool or queue should handle an asset job without paying a frontier LLM to generate prose every time. The architecture and permissive licence are worth testing; the project’s own numbers argue for fine-tuning and evaluation on your actual labels before using it for unattended decisions.

05 · Copyright / creative business

OpenAI and Anthropic ask Australia for a narrower route to train on copyrighted work.

In submissions to Australia’s parliamentary AI inquiry, OpenAI and Anthropic asked the government to reconsider its refusal to create a training-data exception for copyrighted Australian creative work. Anthropic says it accepts that a broad text-and-data-mining exemption is off the table but wants a narrower conditional framework; OpenAI is also arguing for rules that allow learning from publicly available information while creating ways for rightsholders to participate.

No exemption has been granted, and the parliamentary committee is expected to report in November. Australian creators and media groups continue to oppose training on their work without consent or compensation.

Why it mattersThis is a concrete policy test for the terms under which creative work can enter training sets. Consent, licensing, payment and auditability matter more to artists than the broad “innovation versus copyright” framing, and Australia is now negotiating those details while AI companies tie their requests to local infrastructure investment.
