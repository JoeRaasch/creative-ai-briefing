01 · Open models / agents

Xiaomi releases MiMo-V2.6 as an MIT-licensed omnimodal agent model.

Xiaomi’s MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL accepts text, images, video and audio, carries a one-million-token context window, and uses a sparse mixture-of-experts backbone with 1.02 trillion total parameters and 42 billion active per token. The weights are published under the MIT licence, with official SGLang and vLLM serving recipes.

Xiaomi reports large gains on coding and agent benchmarks, and Artificial Analysis currently places Pro at the top end of its open-weight index. The model is not a casual workstation install: Xiaomi’s reference deployment uses eight-way tensor parallelism in vLLM or a 16-way, two-node SGLang configuration.

Why it mattersThe useful combination for creative-agent research is permissive licensing plus native image, video and audio understanding. Xiaomi has demonstrated Blender scene building, multi-agent game creation and video workflows, but those demos are examples of capability rather than evidence that the system is ready to automate production unattended.

02 · 3D / game development

Meshy’s Mora prototype separates world logic, 3D structure and video rendering.

Meshy has detailed Mora, an early research architecture in which coding agents handle game logic, Meshy’s 3D generation supplies assets and spatial structure, and a real-time video model renders the final imagery and audio. Mora 1 is publicly playable, but Meshy describes a general-purpose world-creation tool as a longer-term goal.

The announcement also expands on Meshy 7.1, released September 16: its paid Ultra 4K mode generates geometry on a 4096³ voxel grid, with raw meshes reaching up to 80 million triangles before simplification. Meshy’s detail-quality comparisons come from its own benchmark and should be read as vendor results.

Why it mattersThe layered design is easier to map onto game and VFX pipelines than an end-to-end neural world simulation: rules and geometry stay explicit while generative video handles presentation. Today, the architecture is more useful to study than the product itself; Mora remains an early research demo.

03 · Animation / MCP

Toonkit exposes animation canvases and saved assets through MCP.

Toonkit’s September 18 update, documented on September 21, lets ChatGPT and Claude call its image, video and long-form animation tools through MCP. The integration can drive Toonkit Canvas and pull in saved characters, backgrounds and props instead of regenerating every shot as an isolated asset.

The service remains a hosted, credit-based production tool; MCP adds an agent interface rather than a local model runtime.

Why it mattersThis is a useful pattern for creative agents: conversation controls a structured workspace that retains reusable assets and shot state. That is more production-oriented than a chatbot that only returns individual images or clips.

04 · Multi-GPU video inference

NVIDIA turns multi-GPU video inference into a single serving endpoint.

TensorRT 11 multi-device inference is now supported through NVIDIA Dynamo-Triton 26.07, allowing one served model instance to own several GPUs behind a single gRPC endpoint. NVIDIA demonstrates the setup with Cosmos 3 Nano video generation, using Ulysses context parallelism across as many as eight GPUs while Diffusers handles the surrounding pipeline.

In NVIDIA’s 1280×720, 189-frame test at 35 denoising steps, end-to-end latency fell from 156.6 seconds on one GPU to 34.2 seconds on eight GPUs. Transformer time scaled faster than the rest of the pipeline, so total speedup was 4.58× rather than linear.

Why it mattersFor a lab or studio with several GPUs, this removes some of the engineering needed to expose distributed generation as a service that artists or tools can call. It is infrastructure for shared on-prem compute, not a consumer-desktop optimization.

05 · Higher education

OpenAI Academy adds separate courses for educators and college students.

OpenAI has expanded Academy with role-based paths for developers, leaders, educators and college students. The educator course covers class planning, activities, assessment and student communication; the student course focuses on study planning, group projects, draft review and career preparation.

Courses include assessments and badges, and institutions can combine them with OpenAI’s AI Foundations material and deployment guide. The content is built around ChatGPT, so it is vendor-specific training rather than a neutral AI-literacy curriculum.

Why it mattersFor faculty, the practical value is in the ready-made exercises and review checkpoints, especially as something to compare with local curriculum. It can supplement broader AI-literacy teaching, but it does not replace discipline-specific work on production practice, sourcing, model limits and assessment.
