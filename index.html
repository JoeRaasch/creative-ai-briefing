01 · Local AI video

FastH3 8-Step V2 gets a much easier path into ComfyUI.

FastVideo’s new MiniMax H3 checkpoint generates synchronized video and audio using eight transformer forwards and an 80%-sparse VSA attention setup. ComfyUI now has ready-made workflows around the release, making the accelerated path far more approachable than earlier FastH3 experiments.

Why it matters to youThis is the first item I’d investigate today. The appeal is not only speed: it is a supported graph you can inspect and adapt rather than another fragile one-off hack. Intel XPU is still the big caveat—published paths focus on NVIDIA and Apple Silicon—so I’d test separately before touching a stable B70 setup.

02 · Creative software

Canva’s ProSuite makes Affinity + Cavalry a surprisingly serious free stack.

Canva’s ProSuite push connects Affinity, Cavalry, Flourish and Leonardo while adding major Affinity features including a Blend Tool, scripting, 3D support, INDD import and AI-assisted scene decomposition. The notable pricing move is that the core Affinity and Cavalry applications remain free, with paid Canva access unlocking additional AI features.

Why it matters to youCavalry becoming free makes it much more interesting for teaching motion design, while Affinity is becoming a credible option for students who cannot justify another Adobe subscription. The emerging scripting/agent layer also fits the larger trend: AI operating real creative tools instead of simply replacing them.

03 · Production workflow

Autodesk demonstrates the AI/VFX pipeline worth teaching.

At Autodesk University, Autodesk used a fictional production to demonstrate rapid 3D exploration, AI-assisted dailies, editable scene assembly in Flow Studio and Maya animation. The key idea is structure: character, performance, camera, environment and animation remain separate, editable parts of the production rather than being flattened into a generated clip.

Why it matters to youThis is a strong way to frame AI in animation/VFX education: generate → preserve structure → direct → revise → finish. It keeps the useful parts of conventional production—camera, blocking, performance and editability—while using AI to shorten the path to a first workable pass.
Autodesk: idea to final frame
04 · Model evaluation

OpenArt Arena asks a better question: which model is best for this creative job?

OpenArt’s new Arena separates evaluations into film, animation, advertising, motion design, video editing, graphic design, product imagery and lip sync. Results come from blind paired comparisons, and OpenArt publishes the methodology and confidence intervals rather than presenting one universal “best model” score.

Why it matters to youThis is much closer to how production decisions are actually made. Instead of asking whether Model A is “better” than Model B, you can shortlist based on the job—editing, identity, motion, film imagery—then run your own controlled tests before spending workstation time or API credits.

05 · Imaging / hardware

OpenAI reportedly buys Glass Imaging—and AI moves upstream into the camera.

The Wall Street Journal reports that OpenAI acquired Glass Imaging, a startup founded by former Apple camera engineers, in a deal valued above $300 million. Glass builds a neural image-signal-processing pipeline that operates on RAW sensor data to reverse optical and sensor limitations rather than merely applying a generative effect after capture.

Why it matters to youThe VFX angle is upstream: neural RAW processing, scene understanding and computational optics could eventually improve phone-based reference capture, photogrammetry and material arriving in post. AI is increasingly part of acquisition as well as generation and finishing.
